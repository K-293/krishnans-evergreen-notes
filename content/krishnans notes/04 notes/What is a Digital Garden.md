Your understanding, judgment, and taste cannot be outsourced to a model.

Because models generate patterns based on past data. While,
* understanding grows from sustained engagement with problems over time.
* Judgment comes from seeing consequences play out. 
* Taste forms through repeated choices, failures, and refinements that are specific to you.

> A model can assist thinking, but it cannot accumulate lived context or responsibility. That accumulation is what personal knowledge is for.

But most personal knowledge systems fail because ideas are captured but never revisited, connected, or allowed to evolve. 

A digital garden treats notes as living things rather than finished artifacts. By keeping thoughts public, loosely structured, and continuously revisable, this setup encourages reflection, cross linking, and long term learning instead of static note hoarding.

## Kinds of notes you will find here

This garden contains different kinds of notes, often overlapping.

1. Some notes are quick captures. Short thoughts, questions, quotes, or fragments written to avoid losing an idea. I call them #zettel notes
2. Some notes are #working-notes. Messy explorations where I try to understand a topic, map connections, or think something through.
3. Some notes are #evergreen-notes. Ideas that have stabilized enough to be useful over time, though they may still change.
4. Some notes are #references. Pointers to books, articles, tools, or external resources with light commentary.

## This note taking system is guided by a few simple principles.

1. Capture comes before clarity. Ideas are written down as they appear, without forcing structure or completeness.
2. Notes are allowed to stay unfinished. Roughness is treated as a feature, not a failure.
3. Connection matters more than organization. Links are added when relationships feel meaningful, instead of sorting notes into fixed hierarchies.
4. Revision is ongoing. Notes are revisited, refined, split, or rewritten as understanding changes.

The system is meant to reduce friction, not enforce discipline. If it becomes hard to use, it is adjusted.

## How to read and use this as a reader

You do not need to read this linearly.
1. Follow links that catch your interest. 
2. Skim freely. Treat notes as snapshots in time rather than final positions.
3. If something feels incomplete or contradictory, that is expected. You are seeing thinking in motion.
4. If parts of this are useful, feel free to adapt the ideas for your own system.
